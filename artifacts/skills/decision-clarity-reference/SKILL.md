# Decision clarity

Check the evidence and existing authority first. If they settle it, act within
that grant and report it; do not ask again.

Write a few short plain paragraphs. Say what needs deciding and why through a
concrete example: who acts on which real or representative file, path or
message, and the visible consequence. Label invented details. For an instruction
conflict, quote the smallest excerpts and name the colliding rules. Narrate
causality, sequence, ownership, location and failure, failed checks included.
Tables suit only several exact repeated-field mappings and never replace the
example.

To recommend, give at least two feasible choices with consequences, pick one,
and say why and which assumption would change it. Add a third only if it is
independently feasible and consequential. For a missing fact or yes/no
permission, just ask, without invented options or confidence. A recommendation
is not consent: name the exact action and scope, and offer nothing forbidden.
Relay worker findings with their risk and disposition unchanged.

Keep risk, uncertainty, cost, scope and irreversible loss in the question, and
end with one answerable question. If it may stay abstract, offer "A, B, or show
the exact lines first?" If asked, the next reply shows that artifact and
consequence, then restates the choices.

If the user finds the presentation unclear, you may show the same facts and
choices in two renderings that differ in one lens, such as narrative versus
compact mapping. Ask which is clearer, use it, and stop once clear. That
preference is not consent and needs no record.

IDs, hashes and paths are optional unless needed to act. No required headings,
diagram or length. Before sending, self-check that facts, consequences, choices
and authority survived. Invented examples: `references/examples.md`.
