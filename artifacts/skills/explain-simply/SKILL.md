# Explain simply

Assume the reader is a capable adult who is new to this topic. Explain the
subject they name. If they invoke the skill on its own, use the selected text
or the subject clearly under discussion. If two subjects are equally plausible
or there is no context, ask one short question to identify the subject.

Start with what the reader came to find out.

- **A topic.** Say what it is or how it works in everyday words.
- **A question someone asked them.** Say what the other person needs to know
  and what their answer will affect.
- **A decision already made.** Say what was chosen and why, as far as the
  available facts show. If the reason is unknown, say so.
- **A choice still open.** Recommend a feasible course and give the practical
  reason. Name the main trade-off or the condition that would change your
  advice. If a missing fact decides it, explain how the answer depends on
  that fact or recommend the smallest check that would settle it. Leave the
  decision with the reader.

Check for the step an expert would silently fill in. Ask yourself, “What
would someone have to know already for this conclusion to follow?” Supply
that fact in the answer. If a technical term matters, explain it the first
time you use it, using words the reader already has.

Use one concrete example when it makes the explanation easier to follow.
Prefer a familiar situation or a detail the reader supplied. Signal an
invented example with a word like “suppose”. Keep the answer in one or a
few short paragraphs, with one idea per paragraph. Name who does what.
Cut padded openings and repeated conclusions. Keep any uncertainty that
could change what the reader does.

**Example.** “The bank didn’t reply. Should the shop try the payment again?”

> Check whether the first payment went through before trying again. The bank
> can take the money even if its reply never reaches the shop.
>
> Suppose someone bought a $20 ticket. A second attempt could charge them
> another $20 for the same ticket. If the bank confirms the first attempt
> failed, try again.
